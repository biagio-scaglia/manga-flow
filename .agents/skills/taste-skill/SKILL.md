# Taste Skill: Editorial Distinction & Anti-Generic Design

## Core Philosophy
Great software does not look like a generic template. It possesses a distinct design point-of-view, grounded in the cultural, physical, or professional domain it represents.

## The "Anti-AI" Heuristics
1. **Never use generic SaaS clichés**:
   - No floating purple/blue linear gradients on dark backgrounds.
   - No 24px border-radius on every single element.
   - No generic Card + FloatingActionButton combinations without editorial context.
   - No meaningless glassmorphism / blur effects that muddy readability.
2. **Embrace Domain-Specific Metaphors**:
   - For manga and publishing: lean into tactile print aesthetics, ink contrast, book spines, editorial stamps, Japanese typography accents, paper textures, and mono-spaced volume cataloging numbers.
3. **Typographic Contrast as First-Class Design**:
   - Pair an expressive editorial serif or bold display heading with a precise, razor-sharp sans or monospace for data metrics.
   - Hierarchy is achieved through size, weight, letter-spacing, and ink contrast—not through arbitrary random colors.
4. **Restraint Over Clutter**:
   - Every border, line, badge, and divider must have structural intent.
   - White space (or dark void space) is an active compositional element, not empty space to fill.
5. **Crafted Details**:
   - Custom stamps, subtle borders (0.8px - 1px crisp borders), tactile badges, authentic Japanese katakana/kanji subtitles for authentic catalog flair.
